## How to Tell the Age of Your Oil Tank: Start With the Label

Every oil tank legally sold in Canada carries a certification label, usually from ULC or CSA. On most tanks it's a metal or foil plate about the size of a playing card, riveted or glued to the top of the tank, one of the end plates, or the area near the fill pipe.

That label is gold. It typically lists:

- The manufacturer (Granby, Roth, and so on)
- The model number and capacity in litres
- The standard the tank was built to
- The date of manufacture, usually shown as a month and year

On newer Granby tanks the date is printed right on the label. Roth double-wall tanks carry a label on the front face with the serial number and production date together. If you can read a date here, you're done. Take two or three clear photos, email one to your broker, and keep the rest with your insurance papers. It's worth noting the label shows the manufacture date, not the install date, but insurers generally accept it because tanks are almost always installed new within months of leaving the factory.

A word of caution before you go hunting. Basements are tight, outdoor tanks can be icy or unstable, and an older tank may be more fragile than it looks. Look with a flashlight and wipe gently with a rag if you must, but don't scrape, grind or wire-brush anything. And a leaning tank on frost-heaved blocks is not something to wrestle with on your own. If anything about the tank looks off, stop there and let a professional take it from that point.

## Reading Serial Numbers and Date Stamps When the Label Is Gone

Labels get painted over, rust away, or simply fall off, especially on outdoor tanks that have stood through a few decades of Maritime weather. The tank usually still has more to tell you.

Check these spots with a good flashlight:

- The top plate. Many steel tanks have the manufacture date stamped or embossed directly into the metal on top, often near a seam.
- The collar around the fill and vent openings, and the upper weld seam.
- Both end plates. Stamps sometimes sit on the side facing the wall, which is exactly why nobody has noticed them in 20 years.

If you find a serial number but no obvious date, don't give up. Most manufacturers encode the production year inside the serial, often in the first or last few digits, and their customer service lines can decode it from a phone call or an emailed photo. We do this regularly for customers who send us a clear picture of whatever markings they can find.

One more trick: check for installer paperwork hiding in plain sight. Installers frequently write the install date on a sticker stuck to the tank, the furnace, or the inside of the electrical panel door. Some fuel companies tag tanks with their own inspection stickers too, and those are dated. Any one of those dates gives your broker something solid to work with.

## Other Ways to Figure Out How Old Your Oil Tank Is

No label, no stamp, no legible serial? Time to work the paper trail. In rough order of usefulness:

1. **Your oil delivery company.** Fuel suppliers around Greater Moncton keep tank records because their own insurance depends on it. Many log the tank's make, size and manufacture date at the first delivery, and some check tanks on a schedule. One phone call can solve the whole mystery.
2. **Your home inspection report.** If you bought the house in the last decade or so, dig out the inspection binder. Inspectors routinely note the tank's age, or at least its label details and visible condition.
3. **The previous owner or the listing agent.** Sellers in New Brunswick are usually asked about the oil tank during a sale, and the listing paperwork sometimes states the tank's year outright.
4. **Your own insurance file.** If a past insurer approved this tank, somebody proved its age once. That documentation may still be sitting in your broker's records.
5. **The furnace.** Tanks and furnaces are often installed together. A furnace data plate showing a 2012 build date doesn't prove the tank matches, but it gives your broker a reasonable anchor and tells you where to hunt for installer stickers.

The tank itself offers hints even without markings. Older tanks tend to be the classic 200 imperial gallon size, roughly 910 litres, resting on welded legs or loose cinder blocks. Newer tanks often have double-bottom construction, factory stands and cleaner welds. None of that proves a date, but a seasoned eye can usually place a tank within a decade, which helps you decide whether chasing records is worth the effort.

## When Age Doesn't Tell the Whole Story

Here's the part most homeowners miss: oil tanks usually fail from the inside out. Condensation collects inside the tank, the water sinks below the oil because it's heavier, and the bottom of the tank quietly corrodes where you can't see it. That's why a tank can look decent from the outside and still be a few millimetres from a very bad day, and why we tell people age is a guideline, not a guarantee. Our article on [how long oil tanks last](/blog/how-long-do-oil-tanks-last/) digs into the full lifespan picture.

Whatever the age turns out to be, walk around your tank once a season and look for:

- [Rust blisters or flaking paint](/blog/rust-on-oil-tank/), especially along the bottom seam
- A damp, oily sheen or a slow drip anywhere on the tank
- A wet spot or stained ground underneath it
- Legs that are sinking, tilting or standing on loose blocks
- A strong oil smell nearby

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What if I can't find a date anywhere on my oil tank?

Work the paper trail first: your oil supplier, your home inspection report and the previous owner are the best bets. If nothing turns up, most insurers will simply treat the tank as past its useful life. At that point replacement is usually the practical answer, since a new tank fixes both the paperwork problem and the real risk sitting behind it.

### How old is too old for an oil tank in New Brunswick?

There's no single provincial expiry date, but most insurers draw the line somewhere between 15 and 25 years for steel tanks, and they judge outdoor tanks more harshly than indoor ones. Fibreglass and double-wall tanks are generally given longer. Condition matters as much as the calendar, though, so a rusty 12-year-old tank can be a bigger problem than a clean 18-year-old one.

### Will my insurance company accept a photo of the label?

Often, yes. Some insurers want more, such as an inspection report from a licensed technician or proof of a recent installation. When we visit, we put the tank's age and condition in writing so you have something official to hand your broker. Ask your broker exactly what they need before the deadline so you only have to go through the process once.

### Does an indoor oil tank last longer than an outdoor one?

Generally, yes. Indoor tanks are spared the freeze-thaw swings and the condensation those swings cause, so they often reach 20 to 25 years, while outdoor steel tanks are usually done in 15 to 20. Along the shore near Shediac and Sackville, salt air shortens outdoor tank life even further. Wherever your tank sits, the seasonal walk-around matters more than the location does.
