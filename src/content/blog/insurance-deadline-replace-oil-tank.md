## Read the Letter Twice Before You Do Anything Else

Insurance letters about oil tanks are not all the same, so the first job is figuring out which kind you got. Most fall into one of three categories.

**A proof of age request.** The insurer wants to know how old your tank is before they will confirm renewal. No replacement is required yet, just documentation.

**An inspection requirement.** The insurer will accept a professional's written assessment of the tank's condition instead of an automatic replacement, at least for now.

**A hard replacement deadline.** The tank is past the insurer's age limit and the letter states a date by which a new tank, and paperwork proving it, must be in place.

Find the actual deadline date printed on the letter and write it somewhere you will see it daily. Vague plans built around "sometime this month" are how homeowners miss dates that were perfectly manageable on day one. If age is the trigger, our guide on [how to tell your oil tank's age](/blog/how-old-is-my-oil-tank/) covers where to find a date stamp or serial number before you call anyone.

## Why the Clock Started Now

The age limit and the deadline in your letter come from your insurer's own underwriting rules. Each company writes its own, which is why two neighbours with tanks of the same age can get very different letters, or one gets a letter and the other doesn't.

The timing isn't random either. The insurance industry's own [Code of Consumer Rights and Responsibilities](https://www.ibc.ca/insurance-basics/how-insurance-works/code-of-consumer-rights-and-responsibilities) says insurers provide renewal terms at least 30 days before a policy expires, and that under normal circumstances they advise customers of changes or cancellation at least 30 days before expiry too. That's a floor, not a fixed number. Some insurers write two or three months ahead, and a letter that arrives close to the 30 day mark leaves the least room.

Knowing where the timing comes from tells you the deadline is a structured notice period, not a scare tactic, and that the date on your letter is the one that counts. Our article on [oil tank age and insurance limits](/blog/oil-tank-age-insurance/) covers the specific age ranges insurers tend to use if you want the fuller picture.

## Call Your Broker Before You Call Anyone Else

Your broker, not your insurance company's call centre, is the person who can tell you exactly what will satisfy this specific letter. Ask three things in that first call.

1. **What is the exact deadline, and is it the policy expiry date or something earlier?** Some letters set a date well before renewal so there is a buffer.
2. **Will an inspection report be accepted in place of full replacement, even temporarily?** That tends to be an easier ask for a tank near the young end of the insurer's limit.
3. **What documentation do they need back, and in what format?** A photo of a new data plate, a written installation record, or a formal certificate are all things different insurers ask for.

Get the answers in writing if you can, even just a follow up email confirming the call. That email is what you hand a new contractor, and it is what protects you if there is ever a dispute about what was actually required.

## If the Deadline Lands in the Middle of Winter

Southeast New Brunswick winters don't pause for insurance paperwork, and if your policy renews in December or January, the letter lands right as the ground freezes. A basement swap is not much affected by the weather outside, but an outdoor tank replacement in a Riverview or Salisbury driveway means the crew is working around snowbanks, ice on the pad, and shorter daylight.

## What If You Genuinely Cannot Meet the Date

Talk to your broker before the deadline, not after it passes. A short extension tied to a booked install date is a much easier ask than one made the week coverage lapses. Some insurers will accept a professional inspection as a stopgap for a borderline tank, though this becomes less likely the further past the typical age range your tank sits.

Switching insurers is possible, but applications for a home with oil heat commonly ask the tank's age, so shopping around may just turn up the same condition from a different company. And no grant program pays to replace an oil tank with another oil tank. The federal Oil to Heat Pump Affordability program, which helped households move off oil entirely, is now closed to new applications, so budgeting for the replacement yourself is the realistic plan.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many days does an insurance letter about an old oil tank usually give me?

There's no single number. The industry's consumer code says insurers give renewal terms at least 30 days before a policy expires, so a month is the usual minimum, but many letters arrive earlier and some set a deadline ahead of the expiry date to leave a buffer. The date printed on your own letter is the one that counts, so confirm it with your broker.

### What if my broker won't give me an extension?

Brokers can only pass along what the underwriter allows, and underwriters are generally firmer the further a tank is past their age limit. Your best leverage is a booked, written estimate showing a specific install date inside or close to the original window, since that gives the insurer something concrete rather than a vague promise.

### Does replacing the tank guarantee my policy will renew?

Replacing the tank resolves the tank-age condition, which is usually the whole issue, but renewal also depends on the rest of your file, like claims history and other coverage questions. Send your broker the installation paperwork as soon as the work is done so there is no gap between the deadline and confirmation.

### Can I switch insurers instead of replacing the tank?

You can apply elsewhere, but insurers commonly ask about the oil tank's age and type, so another company may set the same condition, charge more, or limit leak coverage. Ask your broker to check the market before you count on it. For many homeowners, replacing the tank ends up the simpler route.

### What proof do I need to send my broker after the tank is replaced?

Ask your broker exactly what they want before the work happens so you only gather it once. Common asks are a photo of the new tank's data plate, a written installation record with the date, and sometimes a receipt showing the old tank was removed and disposed of properly.

### Does a government grant cover the cost of a new oil tank?

No. Grant programs aimed at heating equipment are built to help homeowners move away from oil heat, not to fund replacing one oil tank with another. If your insurer is requiring a straight tank swap, that cost comes out of pocket, which is why getting a written estimate early in your deadline window matters.
