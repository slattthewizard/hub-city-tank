## How a Roth tank is built

A Roth double wall oil tank is two tanks in one. The inner tank holds the oil. It's blow-moulded high density polyethylene formed in one piece, so there's no welded seam for condensation and sludge to work on the way they do inside a welded steel tank.

Around that inner tank sits a weld-free galvanized steel outer tank. Roth says the outer tank can hold 110 percent of the primary tank, so if the inner tank ever fails, the oil stays inside the steel shell instead of reaching your basement floor, a floor drain, or the ground outside. Roth's [double wall heating oil tank page](https://www.roth-america.com/product/oil-storage-tanks/double-wall-heating-oil-tank/) lists that construction, along with a few details worth knowing:

- An optical leak alarm you can see at a glance, which shows whether oil has got into the space between the two tanks.
- All connections on top. There are no fittings below the oil line, so a weeping fitting can't drain the tank from underneath.
- Light weight. Roth says its tanks are up to 50 percent lighter than conventional steel tanks, which matters on a narrow basement stair.

That's the whole idea behind the brand. It doesn't try to make steel resist rust better. It takes the rusting part out of the oil's path and gives any failure somewhere safe to go. ## Roth tank sizes and dimensions

Roth's residential double wall line comes in five models. The model names use litres; the gallon figures on Roth's spec table are US gallons, not the imperial gallons older Canadian tanks were labelled in.

| Model | Capacity | Length x width x height | Minimum headroom | Empty weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DWT 400L | 400 L (110 US gal) | 74 x 72 x 112 cm | 125 cm | 48 kg |
| DWT 620L | 620 L (165 US gal) | 74 x 72 x 155 cm | 168 cm | 60 kg |
| DWT 1000L | 1,000 L (275 US gal) | 110 x 72 x 155 cm | 168 cm | 76 kg |
| DWT 1000LH | 1,000 L (275 US gal), low height | 130 x 76 x 137 cm | 152 cm | 94 kg |
| DWT 1500L | 1,500 L (400 US gal) | 163 x 77 x 173 cm | 193 cm | 151 kg |

Figures from Roth's product page, rounded to the centimetre. "Minimum headroom" is the clear height Roth lists for each model.

For comparison, a classic 910 litre oval steel tank is about 152 cm long and 58 cm wide; Granby's [EcoGard spec sheet](https://www.granbyindustries.com/products/ecogard-double-bottom-oil-tank/) lists its 910 litre model at 60 x 23 x 47 inches. So the Roth 1000L holds a bit more oil on a shorter footprint (Roth quotes about 8 square feet for it), but at 155 cm it stands about 35 cm taller than that 47 inch (119 cm) steel tank. That trade is the main thing to measure for.

The 1000L is the closest match to the 910 litre tank most Southeast New Brunswick houses grew up with. The 1000LH gives up some footprint to come down in height, which helps under a low beam or duct run. The 620L suits a smaller house or a tank that's now backup to a heat pump. The 1500L is more than most single family homes need.

## What the Roth warranty says

Roth's product page advertises a 30 year warranty "with 10 years insurance, $2 million clean up policy," and adds that the 30 years applies when the tank is installed by a Roth Certified Installer. The full terms are in Roth's [limited warranty certificate](https://www.roth-america.com/app/uploads/2021/02/Warranty-Roth-DWT-Heating.pdf), which says it's valid for tanks installed in the United States and Canada on or after January 1, 2012. This is Roth's warranty, backed by Roth, not a promise we make.

Here's what the certificate says, in plain terms:

- **What's covered for 30 years:** leaks caused by defects in materials and workmanship in the tank itself. Roth will repair it, replace it, or credit a replacement.
- **The first 10 years:** Roth also pays labour on a warranty repair or replacement, up to US$1,000 per case, and reimburses on-site cleanup and damage to your property caused by a leak from a defective tank, up to US$2 million. Cleanup of neighbouring land and groundwater is excluded.
- **What isn't covered:** the fill, vent and return piping, seals, multi-tank connections and other accessories, and damage from improper handling, installation or filling.
- **Conditions that void it:** installation by anyone other than a certified installer, moving the tank from where it was first installed, or not returning the completed warranty certificate within 30 days of installation.
- **If something goes wrong:** Roth asks you to call its customer line right away and give written notice within 10 days of discovering the leak.
- **Selling the house:** coverage stays with later owners, but only while the tank stays at its original site.

Two things follow from that. First, ask whoever quotes your job whether they're a Roth Certified Installer, and get it in writing. Second, make sure the warranty certificate is filled in, sent back, and a copy kept with your house papers. An insurer or a buyer's lawyer asking about the tank years from now will want to see it, and a warranty nobody registered is hard to use.

The warranty covers the tank, not the installation around it. The stand, the lines, the fill and vent, and where the tank sits all fall under the installation code, which we cover in our guide to [CSA B139 oil tank rules](/blog/csa-b139-oil-tank-code/).

## Where a Roth tank fits in a Moncton area home

The tall shape and the sealed outer tank change where a Roth makes the most sense compared with a traditional steel tank.

**Indoors, where floor space is tight.** A Roth 1000L takes less floor than an oval steel tank of similar capacity, which matters in older bungalows and farmhouses around Moncton, Dieppe, and Riverview where the mechanical room was never planned around a tank. Check the headroom column in the table before you settle on a model. Our [indoor vs outdoor oil tanks](/blog/indoor-vs-outdoor-oil-tank/) guide covers the wider trade-offs between the two placements.

**Outdoors, including coastal spots near Shediac.** Roth's product page lists a cover for outside use, so ask your installer what the installation manual requires for an outdoor Roth before you commit. The inner tank can't rust, and the galvanized outer tank has no welded seams. It's still steel on the outside, though, so salt air off the Northumberland Strait will work on it over the years, and it needs a solid pad, a spot clear of roof edges where snow and ice slide down, and a yearly look like any outdoor tank.

**Homes being sold or changing insurers soon.** A new tank with a registered warranty and a leak indicator gives an insurer or a buyer's inspector clear answers to the age and containment questions. Remember that the Roth warranty follows the tank to the next owner only while it stays where it was installed.

Where a Roth makes less sense: a dry basement in a house you plan to sell within a few years, where a new steel tank often meets the insurer's requirement for less money. That call, and the fibreglass alternative, is what our [steel vs fibreglass oil tanks](/blog/steel-vs-fiberglass-oil-tank/) piece walks through, and our [Granby vs Roth](/blog/granby-vs-roth-oil-tanks/) comparison lines the two brands up side by side.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What makes a Roth oil tank different from a regular steel tank?

A Roth is a double wall design: a one-piece polyethylene inner tank that can't rust, inside a galvanized steel outer tank that Roth says holds 110 percent of the inner tank's contents. A regular single wall steel tank has one welded shell, so a leak goes straight to your floor or the ground. On a Roth, the outer tank is there to catch that leak, and a leak indicator shows it.

### How long does a Roth oil tank last?

Roth's warranty runs 30 years from installation when a Roth Certified Installer puts the tank in, covering leaks from defects in materials and workmanship. The polyethylene inner tank doesn't suffer the internal rust that ends most steel tanks. That said, fittings, lines, the filter and the pad age on their own schedule, and they're what usually needs attention first on any tank.

### What sizes does Roth make, and will one fit my basement?

Roth lists five residential models: 400, 620 and 1,000 litres, a low height 1,000 litre version, and 1,500 litres. Most are taller than a classic oval steel tank but take less floor. The 1000L needs about 168 cm of headroom and the low height 1000LH about 152 cm, so measure your ceiling, beams and stairwell before you choose.

### Do I need a Roth certified installer for the warranty?

Yes. Roth's warranty certificate says its obligations don't apply if the tank was installed by anyone other than Roth or an authorized installer, and the completed certificate has to go back to Roth within 30 days of installation. Ask any contractor quoting a Roth whether they hold that certification, and keep your copy of the certificate.

### Is a Roth tank worth the extra cost over steel or fibreglass?

It depends on where the tank sits and how long you'll stay. Tight basements, outdoor and coastal spots, and owners planning to stay many years tend to favour a double wall tank like the Roth. A dry indoor basement in a house you're selling soon often doesn't need the extra spend. The comparisons linked above walk through it side by side.
