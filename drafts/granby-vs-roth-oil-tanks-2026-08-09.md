## Granby Tanks: Proven Steel at a Working Price

Granby has been building oil tanks in Saint-Paul-d'Abbotsford, Quebec since the 1950s, and there's a decent chance the old tank in your basement right now is one of theirs. The standard residential model holds 910 litres, which is the familiar oval tank most of us grew up with.

A modern Granby is not the same tank your parents had, though. Today's models use heavier gauge steel, better welds, and improved coatings. Granby also makes double-bottom tanks, sold under their 2-in-1 line, which add a second layer of steel under the belly of the tank where corrosion usually strikes first.

That last point matters, because steel tanks almost never rust from the outside in. They rust from the inside out. Condensation forms inside the tank, water sinks below the oil because it's heavier, and that layer of water quietly eats the floor of the tank for years before anything shows. That's why a steel tank can look mint on the outside and still fail. We cover the full timeline in our guide to [how long oil tanks last](/blog/how-long-do-oil-tanks-last/).

Here's what Granby gets you:

- **Lower cost.** A standard Granby runs $2,600 to $3,200 installed in the Moncton area, including removal of the old tank in most cases.
- **Proven design.** Techs know these tanks inside and out. Parts, gauges, and fittings are everywhere.
- **Decent lifespan.** A properly installed Granby in a dry basement typically gives you 15 to 25 years.
- **Insurance acceptance.** Nearly every insurer will happily cover a brand new Granby, especially a double-bottom model.

The trade-off is simple. It's still steel, and steel plus water equals rust. The clock starts ticking the day it's installed. Warranties on standard models are typically around 10 years, longer on double-bottom versions, so ask for the current warranty sheet before you sign anything.

## Roth Tanks: Double-Wall Protection Insurers Love

Roth took a completely different approach. The inner tank is seamless, blow-moulded polyethylene, which cannot rust. Period. That inner tank sits inside a leak-proof galvanized steel outer shell that's designed to catch and hold at least 110 percent of the tank's contents if the inner tank ever fails.

That outer containment is the whole story. With a single-wall steel tank, a failure means oil on your basement floor, in your sump, maybe into the ground under your foundation. With a Roth, a failure means oil sitting safely in the outer shell while you call for service. An optical leak indicator shows you at a glance whether anything has ever escaped the inner tank.

The other things homeowners like:

- **Long warranty.** Roth backs its residential tanks with a 30-year limited warranty, and the warranty has historically included clean-up insurance coverage. Confirm the current terms with your installer.
- **Compact footprint.** Roth tanks are taller and narrower than the classic oval, which frees up basement space. The common residential sizes are 620, 1000, and 1500 litres.
- **No internal corrosion worries.** Polyethylene doesn't care about condensation.
- **Insurance leverage.** Some insurers offer easier approvals, and occasionally better terms, for double-wall tanks. If your insurer is being difficult, a Roth often ends the conversation.

The trade-off is the price. Expect $3,900 to $4,800 installed in Greater Moncton, depending on size and how much piping needs to change. If you want the deeper dive on how containment tanks work, we wrote one here: [double-wall oil tanks explained](/blog/double-wall-oil-tanks/).

## Our Honest Take: How to Choose

After enough of these kitchen table conversations, the decision usually comes down to four questions.

**How long are you staying?** If you're selling within five to eight years, a new Granby satisfies the insurer, satisfies the buyer's home inspector, and keeps $1,500 in your pocket. If this is your forever home, the Roth's 30-year warranty means you'll likely never buy another tank.

**Where does the tank live?** Indoor, dry basement, off the concrete on a proper stand? Steel does fine. Outdoor tank, coastal air in Shediac or Cap-Pelé, or a damp cellar? The Roth's construction earns its price out there.

**What does your insurer say?** Call them before you order anything. Some companies are happy with any new tank. Others quietly favour double-wall containment, and a few will even adjust terms for it. Ten minutes on the phone can settle the whole debate for you.

**What does your budget say?** A working furnace with a safe new Granby beats an empty spot where a Roth should go. Don't let perfect be the enemy of warm.

One caution, and we mean it. Tank replacement is not a DIY job in New Brunswick. Oil tank installations must follow the CSA B139 installation code, and the province regulates petroleum storage under the [Petroleum Product Storage and Handling Regulation](https://laws.gnb.ca/en/document/cr/87-97). Improper installs can void your warranty, your insurance, or both, and a botched oil line connection can put hundreds of litres of oil into your basement. In the U.S., the EPA's [storage tank program](https://www.epa.gov/ust) exists precisely because leaked heating oil is so expensive to clean up, and the physics don't change at the border. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a Roth tank worth the extra $1,200 to $1,600 over a Granby?

It depends on where the tank sits and how long you're staying. For outdoor tanks, coastal homes, damp basements, or owners planning to stay 10+ years, the Roth's rust-proof inner tank and 30-year warranty usually justify the premium. For a dry basement in a house you'll sell within a few years, a new Granby double-bottom tank is the smarter spend.

### How long do Granby and Roth oil tanks last in New Brunswick?

A properly installed Granby steel tank typically lasts 15 to 25 years indoors, less for outdoor tanks near salt air. Roth tanks carry a 30-year limited warranty and the polyethylene inner tank can't rust, so they routinely outlast their steel cousins. In both cases, installation quality and moisture matter more than the badge on the tank.

### Will my insurance company accept a new single-wall Granby tank?

Most insurers in New Brunswick will cover a brand new Granby without fuss, especially the 2-in-1 double-bottom models. That said, a handful of companies now prefer or require double-wall containment, so call your broker before ordering. Keep your installation certificate and warranty paperwork, because you'll need them at every renewal.

### Can I install a Granby or Roth oil tank myself?

No, and not just because we say so. New Brunswick regulates oil tank installations under provincial petroleum storage rules and the CSA B139 code, and insurers routinely deny claims on tanks that weren't installed by a licensed professional. A DIY install risks your warranty, your coverage, and your basement. Get a licensed installer and get the paperwork.
