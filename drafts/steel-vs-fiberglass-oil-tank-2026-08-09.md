## Steel Oil Tanks: Pros and Cons

Steel is the default in this part of Canada. Walk down any street in Moncton's older neighbourhoods and most basements and side yards have a steel tank, usually a Granby, sitting on a stand.

**The pros are simple.** Steel is the cheapest way to get a new, insurable tank. Every heating contractor in Westmorland County knows how to install one, parts are everywhere, and a modern double-bottom steel tank is a far better product than the tank your parents had. Steel also shrugs off bumps that would worry a fibreglass owner. A snow shovel, a wheelbarrow, a kid's bike, none of it matters.

**The big con is rust, and it works backwards from what you'd expect.** Steel tanks almost never rust through from the outside first. They corrode from the inside out, where condensation collects at the bottom of the tank and mixes with sludge. By the time you see rust or a wet spot under the tank, the floor of that tank may be paper thin. That's why a steel tank can look fine on the outside and fail the same winter.

Modern steel tanks fight back in two ways. Double-bottom models put a second layer of steel under the oil, with a leak-detection port between the layers. And better internal coatings slow the condensation attack. Even so, most insurers in New Brunswick want steel tanks replaced somewhere between 15 and 25 years, and plenty now draw the line at 15 for single-wall models.

Expected lifespan: 15 to 20 years for single-wall steel, 20 to 25 for double-bottom, sometimes more with good luck and dry conditions. Our post on how long oil tanks last digs into why two identical tanks can age a decade apart.

## Fibreglass Oil Tanks: Pros and Cons

Fibreglass tanks solve steel's one big weakness: they cannot rust. The tank is moulded from resin and glass fibre, so the condensation sitting at the bottom of every oil tank in the Maritimes has nothing to eat.

**The pros stack up over time.** No corrosion means no anode, no internal coating to fail, and no slow inside-out surprise. Most fibreglass tanks sold in Canada carry warranties in the 30-year range, roughly triple a standard steel warranty. They're also lighter than steel, which helps when the tank has to go down basement stairs. And for coastal properties, fibreglass sidesteps the salt-air corrosion that chews through outdoor steel tanks near the Strait. We covered that problem in detail in [salt air and oil tank corrosion](/blog/salt-air-oil-tank-corrosion/).

Marc owns a cottage in Shediac, about 900 metres from the water. His first steel tank lasted barely 12 years outside, with rust blooming along every seam by year eight. When it came due, he paid $4,300 for a fibreglass tank installed on a proper pad. That's roughly $1,400 more than a steel replacement would have cost him, but he doesn't expect to buy another tank in his lifetime.

**The cons are real, though.** Price is the obvious one, usually $1,000 to $1,800 more than comparable steel once it's installed. Fibreglass is also more brittle. It handles oil and weather beautifully but doesn't love hard impacts, so placement and a stable base matter more. A shifting or heaving pad that a steel tank would tolerate can stress a fibreglass shell, which is why we're fussy about the [stand and pad work](/oil-tank-replacement-moncton/) on every fibreglass install. Finally, fewer local suppliers stock them, so in a mid-January emergency you can usually get a steel tank same-day while fibreglass may mean a short wait.

One note so the options are complete: there's a third category, the Roth-style tank with a polyethylene inner tank and a galvanized steel outer shell. It competes hard with fibreglass on lifespan and price. We compare the two most common brands head to head in [Granby vs Roth oil tanks](/blog/granby-vs-roth-oil-tanks/).

## Lifespan, Insurance, and Resale: What Each Tank Means Down the Road

The purchase price is the small number. The tank you choose keeps making financial decisions for you for decades.

**Insurance is the biggest one.** New Brunswick insurers have tightened steadily on oil heat. Many now demand proof of tank age at every renewal, refuse single-wall steel past 15 or 20 years, and some offer better terms for double-bottom or fibreglass tanks. A fibreglass tank with a 30-year warranty is the easiest renewal conversation you'll ever have. Whatever you install, keep the invoice and the installation certificate somewhere safe.

**Leaks are the disaster case.** Heating oil in soil is treated seriously by environmental regulators across North America because even small releases contaminate soil and groundwater and are expensive to remediate. The U.S. EPA's storage tank program documents how far a small petroleum leak can travel underground ([epa.gov/ust](https://www.epa.gov/ust)), and the same chemistry applies on this side of the border. In New Brunswick, a residential spill means a mandatory report, an environmental consultant, and excavation billed by the tonne.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much more does a fibreglass oil tank cost than steel?

In the Greater Moncton area, expect to pay $1,000 to $1,800 more installed. A typical double-bottom steel tank runs $2,700 to $3,600 installed, while fibreglass usually lands between $3,500 and $4,800. Spread over the tank's longer lifespan, the annual cost of the two options is nearly identical.

### Do fibreglass oil tanks really last longer than steel?

Yes, and it's not close. Fibreglass can't corrode, which removes the main failure mode of steel tanks, the slow inside-out rust caused by condensation. Most fibreglass tanks carry warranties around 30 years, versus 10 years on typical single-wall steel. Physical damage and a poor base are the main risks to watch instead.

### Will my insurance company prefer one type of tank?

Most New Brunswick insurers care first about age and construction, and they generally view fibreglass and double-bottom steel more favourably than single-wall steel. Some tighten coverage on single-wall tanks after 15 to 20 years. Always keep your installation certificate and invoice, because proof of age is what renewals turn on.

### Can a fibreglass tank sit outside through a New Brunswick winter?

Yes, fibreglass handles our freeze-thaw cycles and coastal salt air better than bare steel does. The key is a proper, stable pad, because frost heave stresses a fibreglass shell more than it stresses steel. A UV-resistant finish and correct venting matter too, so have it installed by someone who works with fibreglass regularly.
