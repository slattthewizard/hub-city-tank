## Where the name comes from

Granby Industries was founded by Conrad Fournier in Granby, Quebec, which is where the name comes from, and the company dates itself to 1954 and calls itself a Canadian leader in storage tanks. Its warranty certificates give its address today as Cowansville, Quebec. Two details from those certificates surprise people: Granby's fibreglass tanks are covered through Granby FRP Tanks Inc. in Waverley, Nova Scotia, and the 2-in-1 double wall tank is manufactured for Granby by Dehoust GmbH, a German tank maker.

None of that changes how you choose a tank. It does explain why the name is so familiar: Granby started out making residential oil tanks and has been at it in Canada for over 70 years, so plenty of older basements already have one.

## The current Granby lineup

Granby's [residential tank lineup](https://www.granbyindustries.com/product-category/residential-tanks/) lists three families. They solve the corrosion problem in different ways.

**EcoGard (double-bottom steel).** A vertical obround steel tank with a second steel bottom and a gap between the two. The gap does one job: it gives a visual leak check at the bottom of the tank, the part most exposed to water and sludge, so a failure of the inner bottom shows up before oil reaches the floor. It has an electrostatic powder-coated finish, a 1/2 inch or 2 inch bottom outlet, and top-draw ready versions. It's certified to ULC-S602, the Canadian standard for aboveground steel tanks for fuel oil. Granby's [EcoGard page](https://www.granbyindustries.com/products/ecogard-double-bottom-oil-tank/) has the full spec sheet.

**2-in-1 (double wall).** The inner tank is blow-moulded high density polyethylene, treated by fluorination to resist fuel odour and vapour, inside a galvanized steel outer tank that forms the secondary containment. A visual indicator shows if the inner tank has leaked. It's certified to UL/ULC-2258, the standard for aboveground non-metallic fuel oil tanks, and Granby approves it indoors or outdoors, with its rain cover mandatory for an outdoor install. The [2-in-1 product page](https://www.granbyindustries.com/products/2-in-1-double-wall-oil-tank/) lists the details. We compare it against Roth's double wall tank in [Granby vs Roth oil tanks](/blog/granby-vs-roth-oil-tanks/), so we won't repeat that here.

**Fibreglass (single wall and double wall).** The only family with no steel in the tank at all: a resin and glass-fibre shell with a UV-resistant gel coat. Granby sells a vertical single wall, a vertical double wall for extra leak protection, and a horizontal single wall that stands only 28 inches tall for tight spaces. All are certified to UL/ULC-2258, and Granby approves the vertical single wall for indoor and outdoor use. We go deeper on fibreglass against steel in [steel vs fibreglass oil tanks](/blog/steel-vs-fiberglass-oil-tank/).

What you won't find in that lineup is the plain single wall steel oval that many local basements still have. Granby's current steel-tank warranty certificate still lists single wall obround tanks, for indoor installation only, but the residential page doesn't show one. If your old tank is a single wall Granby, the year on its label is what your insurer will look at first.

A few dimensions from Granby's spec tables help with the fit question. The 910 litre EcoGard is 60 inches long, 23 inches wide and 47 inches tall, close to the classic oval tank it usually replaces. The 1,000 litre 2-in-1 is 44 by 28 inches on the floor but 64 inches tall. Granby says the 2-in-1 suits basements with as little as 60 inches of headroom and doorways only 28 inches wide, but that fits the smaller 720 litre model (48 inches tall), not the 1,000. The horizontal fibreglass tank goes the other way: low and long.

So measure before you pick a model: ceiling height under beams and ducts, the stairwell, and every doorway on the way in. If you're not sure a model will clear, sort it out on a site visit rather than from a spec sheet.

## What Granby's warranty covers

Every Granby warranty is between you and Granby, not us, so read this as what the manufacturer states, not a promise we're making. Granby's own [warranty page](https://www.granbyindustries.com/warranties/) says the official document for your exact model, certification, country and installation date is what governs, and those documents are posted on each product page.

What the warranty page and certificates say:

- **No registration.** Coverage starts automatically on the installation date. Keep the installation invoice and the tank's serial number.
- **Claims go through your installer.** You give the model, serial number and installation details to your contractor, who takes the claim to a Granby distributor.
- **Later owners are covered, with limits.** Per the certificates, coverage passes to later owners only while the tank stays at its original installation address, and only for tanks installed in Canada.
- **Labour and freight.** Granby's page says labour, freight, handling, removal and reinstallation are generally not covered unless the document says so.

The steel and non-metallic certificates differ in one way that matters. Granby's [ULC-S602 steel tank warranty](https://www.granbyindustries.com/app/uploads/2026/04/WARR.034.CE_.0423_Granby-ULC-S602-Warranty.pdf) (tanks installed in Canada after March 31, 2023) covers EcoGard tanks for 25 years against leaks from defects in materials and workmanship, but a note on the coverage table says leakage or damage caused by corrosion perforating the steel, inside or out, is not covered. The UL/ULC-2258 certificate for fibreglass and 2-in-1 tanks covers leaks from manufacturing defects for 30 years, and for the 2-in-1 it also covers corrosion perforation of the galvanized outer tank when it's caused by a manufacturing defect. Both certificates also exclude damage caused by transferring oil from an old tank into the new one, and spills from overfills. Ask your installer how they handle the old oil before the swap.

Older tanks may fall under different paper. The EcoGard page, for instance, posts a separate warranty document for EcoGard tanks installed before January 2011, so the number on an old brochure isn't necessarily the number on your tank.

That brings us to the label. The ULC label on a tank normally shows the maker, capacity, serial number and year of manufacture, and it's the fastest way to answer the age question your insurer will ask. We cover where to find it, and what to do if it's gone, in [how to tell the age of your oil tank](/blog/how-old-is-my-oil-tank/).

## Steel, double wall or fibreglass: which Granby fits your house

Nobody needs the most expensive tank on the list. Here's roughly how the choice breaks down.

An **EcoGard** makes sense for a dry indoor basement where budget matters. The double bottom gives you an early warning at the spot steel tanks usually fail. Just remember its warranty covers manufacturing defects, not rust.

A **2-in-1** makes sense if your insurer is asking for secondary containment, if headroom and doorways are tight (the 720 litre model especially), or if you want an inner tank that can't rust. It's Granby's answer to the Roth-style tank.

**Fibreglass** earns its higher price on tanks that sit outside, on coastal properties, or in a house you plan to keep a long time, since there's no steel to rust. ### What's the difference between EcoGard and the 2-in-1?

EcoGard is a double-bottom steel tank: two layers of steel at the bottom with a gap that lets you spot a leak before it reaches the floor. The 2-in-1 is a polyethylene inner tank that can't rust, sitting inside a galvanized steel outer tank that contains a leak. Both are Granby products, but they deal with corrosion in opposite ways, and their warranties differ.

### Do I need to register my Granby tank to activate the warranty?

No. Granby's warranty page says coverage starts automatically on the installation date, with no registration. Keep the installation invoice and the tank's serial number from the label, since that's what your installer will need to bring a claim to a Granby distributor. Coverage stays with the tank only at its original address.
