# Canada Greener Homes Grant Update: What's Left for New Brunswick Homeowners

If you're searching for the Canada Greener Homes Grant, here's the update: it's closed. Applications ran from May 2021 to February 2024, and the last day for existing applicants to upload their documents was December 31, 2025. The companion Canada Greener Homes Loan closed to new applications on October 2, 2025, and its funding is now fully committed. Neither one is taking new applicants.

That doesn't mean there's nothing left. A federal successor program is rolling out province by province, a separate federal program for oil-heated homes has its own (also closed) timeline, and New Brunswick runs its own efficiency programs through NB Power's Save Energy NB. This update sorts out what's still open, what isn't, and where an aging oil tank fits no matter which program you're looking at. ## The Short Version

Three federal programs get mixed up constantly: the Canada Greener Homes Grant, the Canada Greener Homes Loan, and the Oil to Heat Pump Affordability program (OHPA). All three are closed to new applications. A fourth, the Canada Greener Homes Affordability Program, is running in some provinces but hasn't reached New Brunswick.

| Program | Status | Key dates |
|---|---|---|
| Canada Greener Homes Grant | Closed to new applicants | Applications ran May 2021 to February 2024. December 31, 2025 was the last day to upload documents; payment requests are no longer accepted. |
| Canada Greener Homes Loan | Closed to new applicants | Portal closed to new applications October 2, 2025 (CMHC). Natural Resources Canada says funding is fully committed. |

## What Happened to the Canada Greener Homes Grant

The Canada Greener Homes Grant paid homeowners toward upgrades like insulation, air sealing and heat pumps, based on an EnerGuide home evaluation. [Natural Resources Canada's closure page](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/energy-efficiency/home-energy-efficiency/canada-greener-homes-initiative/closed-canada-greener-homes-grant-0) says applications were open from May 2021 to February 2024. By the program's own count, more than 409,000 households made energy-saving upgrades, the average grant was about $4,430, the program paid out $1.8 billion in grants overall, and more than 230,000 heat pumps went in.

If you applied before the window closed, the deadlines have passed for you too. The same page says December 31, 2025 was the final day for applicants to upload their documents, and requests for payment are no longer accepted. If you're only hearing about the grant now, there's no way in. The page doesn't talk about reopening; it points people to other programs instead, starting with the Canada Greener Homes Affordability Program covered below.

## The Canada Greener Homes Loan Is Also Closed

The loan side was interest-free financing of up to $40,000, repaid over 10 years, for retrofits an energy advisor had recommended. [Natural Resources Canada's loan page](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/energy-efficiency/home-energy-efficiency/canada-greener-homes-initiative/canada-greener-homes-loan) is now marked closed and says funding for the loan is fully committed and new loan applications can't be approved. [CMHC's loan page](https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/funding-programs/all-funding-programs/canada-greener-homes) gives the date: the portal closed to new applications as of October 2, 2025. Homeowners who already had a loan approved keep their funding and can carry on with their retrofit. Nobody new gets in.

For a few years the usual way to pay for a heat pump retrofit was grant money up front and the loan for the rest, interest free. With the grant closed to applications since early 2024 and the loan portal shut since October 2025, that pairing is done.

## What Replaces Them: CGHAP, Not Yet Here

The federal successor is the Canada Greener Homes Affordability Program, usually shortened to CGHAP. Instead of paying homeowners back after the work, [the program page](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/energy-efficiency/home-energy-efficiency/canada-greener-homes-initiative/canada-greener-homes-affordability-program) says it provides no-cost retrofits, such as insulation and heat pumps, to low-to-median-income homeowners and tenants. It uses a direct-install approach: organizations selected in each province plan the work and cover the costs, and the Government of Canada isn't taking applications from households itself. The page also says the program is moving over to Environment and Climate Change Canada.

The catch for Southeast New Brunswick: as of late September 2026, New Brunswick isn't on the page's list of provinces where residents can apply. British Columbia, Manitoba, Nova Scotia, Prince Edward Island and Quebec each have a provincial program running CGHAP. The page says links to more provincial and territorial programs will be added as they become available. If CGHAP comes to New Brunswick, it will run through a provincial program, not a form on the federal site, so watch for that announcement rather than trying to apply federally.

## What About the Oil to Heat Pump Program?

The Oil to Heat Pump Affordability program (OHPA) gets confused with the grant because both paid toward heat pumps. OHPA was aimed only at oil-heated homes, and it's closed too. The two official pages list different dates, and the reason matters for a Moncton-area file.

In New Brunswick, NB Power delivered OHPA on behalf of Natural Resources Canada through its Save Energy NB programs. [Save Energy NB's OHPA page](https://www.saveenergynb.ca/for-home/oil-to-heat-pump-affordability/) says registration closed June 30, 2026. If you enrolled before July 1, 2026, all the paperwork for the advance payment had to be in by October 1, 2026, and all upgrades plus the final energy evaluation must be finished by March 31, 2027. Miss that last date and any advance you received has to be paid back to NB Power.

[The federal OHPA page](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/energy-efficiency/home-energy-efficiency/canada-greener-homes-initiative/oil-heat-pump-affordability-program) lists July 31, 2026 as the last day to apply and January 31, 2027 as the deadline to upload final receipts and the attestation form. But the top of that page says its information is for applicants in Alberta, Manitoba, the Northwest Territories, Quebec and Saskatchewan, and it sends New Brunswick residents to their provincial program. So for a New Brunswick homeowner, the Save Energy NB dates are the ones to plan around. Better still, follow the dates in your own approval letter or emails, since those were written for your file.

One OHPA detail is worth knowing even though it's closed. Both pages list the safe removal of your oil tank as an eligible cost under the program, and the federal page adds that OHPA doesn't require removal but the cost of removing the tank counts as an eligible expense. On the program pages linked in this update, that's the only place tank removal shows up, and it was tied to switching the house to a heat pump, never to putting in another oil tank.

## What a Southeast NB Homeowner Can Still Use

With the grant, the loan and OHPA all closed to new applicants, and CGHAP not running in this province yet, what's left for a Moncton, Dieppe or Riverview homeowner comes from NB Power's Save Energy NB programs rather than a federal form. The Save Energy NB OHPA page points oil-heated households to two of them:

- Households with incomes of $70,000 and under may be eligible for free energy efficiency upgrades through the Enhanced Energy Savings Program, including help switching off oil heat.
- Households with incomes over $70,000 can still get rebates of up to $2,000 on mini-split, air source, geothermal, multi-zone and furnace add-on heat pumps through the Total Home Energy Savings Program, which starts with a home energy evaluation.

Neither program, as Save Energy NB describes it, mentions oil tank removal. Eligibility rules and amounts can change, so confirm them with Save Energy NB before you plan a budget around any figure.

None of this changes one fact: none of these programs pays to replace an oil tank with another oil tank. They fund efficiency upgrades and moves away from oil heat, not new oil equipment. If your tank is old, leaking or flagged by an insurer, that decision sits outside the grant conversation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is the Canada Greener Homes Grant still open?

No. Applications were open from May 2021 to February 2024, and December 31, 2025 was the last day for existing applicants to upload their documents. Natural Resources Canada says requests for payment are no longer accepted. If you never applied, there's no way in, and the closure page points people to other programs rather than to any reopening.

### Can I still apply for the Canada Greener Homes Loan?

No. The loan portal closed to new applications on October 2, 2025, according to CMHC, and Natural Resources Canada says loan funding is now fully committed and new applications can't be approved. Homeowners who were already approved keep their funding and can finish their retrofit. New applicants have no route in.

### What replaced the Canada Greener Homes Grant?

The federal successor is the Canada Greener Homes Affordability Program, which pays for no-cost retrofits for low-to-median-income households through provincial programs rather than a federal application. As of late September 2026, New Brunswick isn't listed among the participating provinces, so the program isn't reachable here yet.

### Is the Oil to Heat Pump Affordability Program the same as the Canada Greener Homes Grant?

No. OHPA was a separate program just for oil-heated homes switching to a heat pump. In New Brunswick it ran through Save Energy NB, and registration closed June 30, 2026. The federal page shows July 31, 2026, but its dates are for the provinces Ottawa served directly. If you're already approved, follow the dates in your own approval letter.

### Will any of these programs pay to replace my oil tank with a new oil tank?

No. None of these programs lists a new oil tank as an eligible cost. The one place tank removal appears is OHPA: both the federal and Save Energy NB pages list safe removal of your oil tank as an eligible cost as part of switching to a heat pump, and OHPA is now closed to new applicants.

### With the grants closed, is it still worth replacing an old oil tank?

That depends on the tank, not the grant calendar. An insurer's letter, a rusted stand or a leaking seam doesn't wait for federal funding to reopen. Call for a free written estimate and you'll get a straight answer on what your tank needs, independent of any program.
