# What Paperwork You Need After a New Oil Tank Installation

A new tank doesn't come with a government-issued certificate in most Southeast New Brunswick homes. What it should come with is a short stack of ordinary paperwork: an installation record, a photo of the tank's label, a note on the warranty, and a receipt showing the old tank left the property properly. That stack is what an insurer, a buyer's lawyer, or an oil supplier will ask to see.

This guide sorts out what New Brunswick does and doesn't require in writing, what a new tank's own paperwork should include, and how to build a folder that answers every question before it's asked.

## Is There an Official Oil Tank Installation Certificate in New Brunswick?

Not for most single-family homes, and it's worth being precise about why, because "no certificate" and "no rules" are two very different claims.

New Brunswick's Petroleum Product Storage and Handling Regulation (Reg 87-97) is the provincial rulebook for petroleum storage tanks. Its paperwork steps have a size threshold, though. The storage licence in Part II and the site approval and environmental approval in Part III apply to a system with a total capacity of 2,000 litres or more (200 litres or more at a marina), and the regulation counts every tank on the same property toward that total. The regulation even has a "residential licence" category, but it only reaches homes over that line. Most home tanks hold about 900 to 1,150 litres. Two 910 litre tanks piped together come to 1,820 litres, still under the line, while two 1,000 or 1,150 litre tanks go over it. For a single tank of ordinary size, the province's licence and approval steps don't apply, so there's no matching government certificate to collect. The regulation's full table of contents is on the province's [laws site](https://laws.gnb.ca/en/tdm/cr/87-97).

That doesn't mean a small tank sits outside the regulation. Reg 87-97 still covers aboveground tank systems of every size in other ways. For example, anyone who suspects a leak has to report it right away. The province's Petroleum Storage Tank Installer's Licence is tied to that approved work above the threshold. [Environment and Climate Change Canada's summary of installer rules by province](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/pollutants/storage-tanks-petroleum-allied-products/certified-installers.html) confirms New Brunswick licenses installers under Reg 87-97. For an ordinary house-sized tank, the installation itself still has to follow CSA B139, the national installation code for oil-burning equipment, which our [CSA B139 guide](/blog/csa-b139-oil-tank-code/) covers on its own. So the paperwork that matters for a typical home isn't a government form. It's the record your installer gives you and the tank's own label.

## What You Should Receive When a New Tank Goes In

The document that matters most is the installation record or invoice from whoever did the work. At minimum it should show the date of installation, the address, the tank's make, model and serial number, its capacity, and what was done, including removal of the old tank if there was one. ## The Tank's Own Label Is Part of Your Paperwork Too

A certified tank carries a label or rating plate, usually on the end or the top, with the standard it was built to (ULC-S602 for a typical steel tank, for example), the manufacturer, the capacity, a serial number and the date of manufacture. That label is effectively a second copy of your tank's identity, separate from the installer's invoice.

Photograph it the week the tank goes in, while it's clean and easy to read, not years later when basement dust or salt air off the Northumberland Strait has dulled it. Our guide on [how to tell the age of your oil tank](/blog/how-old-is-my-oil-tank/) covers exactly where to look and how to read a serial number if the printed date has worn off. Between the label photo and the installer's invoice, you've got two independent sources for the same facts, which is exactly what an insurer wants to see when either one is questioned.

## Manufacturer Warranty: Register It, or Just File the Receipt?

Warranty terms vary by brand, so check the one for your own tank rather than assuming. Granby, a brand you'll see on plenty of tanks in this region, states on its [warranty page](https://www.granbyindustries.com/warranties/) that its tanks are covered automatically from the installation date and that no registration is required. What it asks you to keep instead is the installation invoice from your certified installer and the tank's serial number, the same two things you should already be filing.

The installation date matters more than most people realize. Granby's [limited warranty certificate for its ULC-S602 steel tanks](https://www.granbyindustries.com/app/uploads/2026/04/WARR.034.CE_.0423_Granby-ULC-S602-Warranty.pdf) has blanks for the model, serial number and date installed, and tells the owner to keep it. It also says that if you can't prove the original installation date, the tank is treated as installed no more than 180 days after it was made. For a tank that sat in a warehouse for a while, that can shave months off your coverage. Write the date in and keep the certificate with the invoice.

If your tank is a different brand, its warranty document will say whether registration is expected. Either way, the safe habit is the same one that protects you with your insurer: keep the invoice and the serial number together, in one place, from day one.

## The Old Tank's Paperwork Matters Just as Much

When a tank comes out, whether it's being replaced or simply removed, ask for a receipt or record confirming it was drained, disconnected and taken away properly. This isn't paperwork you'll need next week. It's paperwork you'll need years from now, when a buyer's inspector notices a patch of old paint on the basement floor or a capped pipe stub outside and asks what used to be there. ## Who Asks to See This Paperwork

Three groups will ask, usually at the least convenient moment.

Your insurer asks first, often at renewal. Age and condition drive most of their questions, and our [oil tank age and insurance](/blog/oil-tank-age-insurance/) guide covers how those age limits play out in practice. Some insurers work from a standardized industry form when they collect this information. CSIO, the industry body that maintains common insurance forms across Canada, publishes a national [Fuel Oil Tank Questionnaire](https://csio.com/fuel-oil-tank-questionnaire) that brokers use for exactly this purpose. It asks for the installation date, whether the installer was a certified oil burner technician, whether the tank was new or used when it went in, the tank's capacity, construction, manufacturer, year made and certification label, plus details on clearances, the fill and vent pipes, and any safety features fitted. Your broker may send it to you or fill it in with you. Either way it's a good preview of the level of detail an insurer might want, and most of those fields are answered by your installation invoice and label photo.

A buyer's lawyer or a buyer's own insurer asks next, if and when you sell. Our guide to [selling a house with oil heat](/blog/selling-house-with-oil-heat/) covers the full paperwork package buyers expect, and a new tank with its invoice, label photo and disposal receipt for the old one is close to the strongest version of that package a seller can hand over.

An oil supplier may ask too. Some companies check the age or condition of a tank before they'll deliver to it, and policies differ from one supplier to the next. If yours asks, a recent installation invoice and the label photo are the quickest way to answer.

## Building Your Oil Tank Paperwork Folder

None of this needs to be complicated. One folder, physical or digital, with five things in it:

| Document | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Installer's invoice or installation record | Proves date, tank details, and what was removed |
| Photo of the tank's certification label | Backs up the invoice with the tank's own data |
| Warranty page or registration confirmation | Shows what's covered and for how long |
| Old tank's removal or disposal receipt | Closes the question of what happened to the tank that left |
| Any inspection or insurer correspondence | Keeps a record of what your insurer has already accepted |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does New Brunswick issue an official oil tank installation certificate?

Not for a typical single-family tank. The licence and approval steps in Reg 87-97 apply to systems with a total capacity of 2,000 litres or more on the property, and most home tanks hold about 900 to 1,150 litres. The regulation still applies to smaller tanks in other ways, such as reporting a leak. For a tank that size, the paperwork that matters is your installer's invoice and the tank's own certification label, not a government form.

### What paperwork does my insurer actually want to see?

Most want proof of the tank's age and condition: the installer's invoice, a photo of the certification label, and sometimes a written inspection. Some brokers work from a standardized industry form, CSIO's Fuel Oil Tank Questionnaire, which asks about installation date, capacity, construction and safety features. Ask your broker directly what they need before a renewal deadline arrives.

### Do I need to register my tank's manufacturer warranty?

It depends on the brand, so check your tank's own warranty document. Granby, for example, states that coverage starts automatically from the installation date with no registration required, and asks owners to keep the installation invoice and the tank's serial number instead. Other manufacturers may ask you to register, so don't assume either way.

### What if I never got an invoice for my current tank?

Start with the tank's own label for the manufacture date and serial number, then check your home purchase documents, a past home inspection report, or your oil supplier's delivery records. If none of that turns up anything and the tank is old, treating it as undocumented and planning a replacement is usually faster than trying to reconstruct decades-old paperwork.

### How long should I hold onto my oil tank paperwork?

For as long as you own the tank, and then hand the folder to whoever buys the house. A new owner who receives the installation invoice, the label photo and the old tank's disposal receipt inherits a tank with a clean history instead of a mystery, which is exactly what their insurer will ask about first.
