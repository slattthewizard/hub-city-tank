# Save Energy NB: What's on Offer If You Heat With Oil

Save Energy NB is the name NB Power puts on a handful of energy efficiency programs, funded with the province and the federal government. If you heat with oil in Moncton, Dieppe or anywhere in Southeast New Brunswick, three of those programs matter: one gives free upgrades to lower income households, one pays rebates after an energy audit, and one, aimed squarely at oil furnaces, is now closed to new applicants.

None of them is simple, and the oil-to-heat-pump part changed in mid-2026. Here is what each program says right now, whether any of them touches your oil tank, and what that means if you are deciding what to do with an aging tank while you wait on a rebate. If a rebate question turns into a tank question, call [(506) 806-0900](tel:+15068060900) for a free written estimate and get that part of the budget settled.

## What Save Energy NB is

Save Energy NB is NB Power's umbrella brand for residential energy efficiency programs, delivered with funding support from the Government of Canada and the Province of New Brunswick. It is not one program with one set of rules. The two that apply broadly are the [Enhanced Energy Savings Program](https://www.saveenergynb.ca/en/for-home/enhanced-energy-savings-program/) and the [Total Home Energy Savings Program](https://www.saveenergynb.ca/en/save-energy/residential/total-home-energy-savings-program/). A third, the [Oil to Heat Pump Affordability program](https://www.saveenergynb.ca/for-home/oil-to-heat-pump-affordability/) (OHPA), was built specifically for households switching off oil, and it closed to new registrations partway through 2026.

## The Enhanced Energy Savings Program: free upgrades below a $70,000 income

The Enhanced Energy Savings Program is the no-cost route. Its page states plainly: if your combined household income is under $70,000, you may qualify for free upgrades. The page also says it's open to eligible homeowners no matter how they heat their home or who their energy provider is, so eligibility turns on income, not fuel type.

The upgrades on offer include basement, attic and wall insulation, ventilation improvements, and energy-efficient heat pumps for heating and cooling. The page says the program can also support a switch away from oil heating as part of those upgrades. If you get a mini-split heat pump through it, the page lists a 10-year warranty on parts and labour from the program, plus three free deep cleans within that period, while regular filter cleaning and keeping snow and ice clear of the outdoor unit stay your job. It does not mention oil tank removal or put a dollar value on the upgrades.

To apply, you register online with a copy of the most recent Notice of Assessment showing line 15000 for each adult in the home, then get an email or letter from NB Power if you're accepted. A pre-upgrade evaluation follows, and the page says the upgrade work itself may happen 2 to 7 months after that evaluation, done by an NB Power-approved contractor of your choice, with a post-upgrade evaluation at the end. It's a slower process than a straight rebate cheque, since the two evaluations bracket the actual job.

## The Total Home Energy Savings Program: audit first, rebates after

If your household income is above the Enhanced program's threshold, the Total Home Energy Savings Program is the other route, and it starts differently: with an initial Home Energy Evaluation by a Certified Energy Advisor, not a free upgrade (ask the service organization what the evaluation costs before you book it). The page's own line is "energy savings from top to bottom, no matter how you heat," and it gives you nine months from the date of that evaluation to complete the recommended work and claim the rebates.

The site describes "up to thousands of dollars in incentives" without listing a single combined maximum, because the total depends on which upgrades your evaluation recommends and which rebate lines you use. One of those lines is [Space Heating Equipment Rebates](https://www.saveenergynb.ca/en/for-home/total-home/rebates/space-heating-equipment-rebates/), and it does list exact figures: $1,500 for a central ducted air source heat pump, $1,000 for a furnace heat pump add-on, $2,000 for a geothermal heat pump, $400 for a single mini-split head, and $350 per indoor head for a multizone system. Each heat pump has to be on NB Power's qualifying list (or meet the listed standard for geothermal and add-on units), so check the model before you sign a quote. The bottom of that rebate page still carries a line about being eligible for up to $15,000 when switching off oil. That was the OHPA advance payment covered below, and Save Energy NB's own OHPA page says registrations for it are closed.

The Total Home page also says efficiency upgrades can help you save up to 25 percent on your mortgage insurance premium. That's a question for your lender or mortgage insurer, not money Save Energy NB pays out.

Nothing on either page names oil tank removal or oil tank replacement as an eligible cost. The audit and the rebate table are built around heating equipment and building envelope, not the tank itself.

## OHPA is closed, and the closing dates do not match

The Oil to Heat Pump Affordability program is the one built for exactly this switch: oil furnace out, heat pump in, income-tested support to help pay for it. It is also the program that explicitly names your oil tank, so it is worth reading closely even though it is closed to new applicants.

Here is the catch. The two official pages that cover it list different dates, and it's worth knowing why. The New Brunswick delivery page says NB Power delivers OHPA here on behalf of Natural Resources Canada through the Save Energy NB programs, and that registration closed June 30, 2026. If you enrolled before July 1, 2026, all paperwork for the advance payment had to be in by October 1, 2026, and all upgrades and the final energy evaluation must be finished by March 31, 2027. Miss that last date and any advance you received has to be repaid to NB Power.

The [federal Natural Resources Canada page](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/energy-efficiency/home-energy-efficiency/canada-greener-homes-initiative/oil-heat-pump-affordability-program) says the last day to apply was July 31, 2026, and that approved applicants must upload final receipts and the attestation form by January 31, 2027. But the top of that page says its information is for applicants in Alberta, Manitoba, the Northwest Territories, Quebec and Saskatchewan, and it sends New Brunswick residents to their provincial program.

For a New Brunswick homeowner, the practical read is: the program is closed either way, so a new application won't get in under either set of dates, and the Save Energy NB dates are the ones written for this province. If you already registered and are working through an approved OHPA file, follow the dates printed in your own approval letter or emails rather than either public page. If your letter is silent on a date, call NB Power's Save Energy NB line and ask which deadline your file falls under.

On the dollar side, the two pages describe different payments. The federal page lists an upfront payment of up to $10,000 for eligible households at or below the median after-tax income, and says that upfront payment applies where the program is delivered only by the federal government. The New Brunswick page, under its heading for households with incomes over $70,000, says registrations for an advance payment of up to $15,000 are now closed, and that rebates of up to $2,000 are still available for mini-split, air source, geothermal, multi-zone and furnace add-on heat pumps. The advance was based on approved contractor quotes: if a project cost more than $15,000, the homeowner paid the difference. Households at $70,000 and under are pointed to the Enhanced program instead. Read your own approval before assuming which figure applies to your file.

## Does any Save Energy NB program pay to remove an oil tank?

Only OHPA does, and only for the households who got in before it closed. The New Brunswick OHPA page specifically lists safe removal of your oil tank as an eligible cost within the program's advance funding, and the federal page says the same, adding that OHPA doesn't require removal but the cost of removing the tank is an eligible expense. That is the one place across these Save Energy NB programs where oil tank removal appears in writing, and it's removal as part of a switch to a heat pump. No program here pays to replace an oil tank with another oil tank.

The Enhanced Energy Savings Program and the Total Home Energy Savings Program, the two programs still open to new applicants, do not mention the tank at all on the pages that describe them. If you are applying to either one today, plan on paying for tank work yourself and treat any efficiency rebate as covering the heating equipment side only, not the tank.

Here is a quick side by side of what each program offers a New Brunswick oil household right now.

| Program | Status for new applicants | Income test | Covers oil tank removal |
|---|---|---|---|

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Save Energy NB?

Save Energy NB is NB Power's name for its residential energy efficiency programs, delivered with funding from the federal and provincial governments. It covers several separate programs, including free upgrades for lower income households, audit-based rebates for other homeowners, and the now-closed Oil to Heat Pump Affordability program for oil furnace conversions.

### Do I qualify for free upgrades under Save Energy NB?

You may, under the Enhanced Energy Savings Program, if your combined household income is under $70,000. That program covers insulation, ventilation and heat pump upgrades at no cost, and a mini-split installed through it comes with the program's 10-year parts and labour warranty and three free deep cleans. Households above that income line use the audit-based Total Home Energy Savings Program instead.

### Does Save Energy NB or OHPA pay to remove my oil tank?

Only the Oil to Heat Pump Affordability program names oil tank removal as an eligible cost, and it is closed to new registrations. The two programs still accepting applications, the Enhanced and Total Home Energy Savings programs, do not mention oil tank removal on their program pages, so budget for that cost separately.

### Is the Oil to Heat Pump Affordability program still accepting applications?

No. New Brunswick's delivery page says registration closed June 30, 2026. The federal page gives July 31, 2026, as the last day to apply, but its dates are for provinces where Ottawa delivered the program directly. Either way, new applications are not being accepted. Homeowners already approved should follow the deadline stated in their own approval letter.

### How much does a heat pump rebate cover in New Brunswick?

Under the Total Home Energy Savings Program's Space Heating Equipment Rebates, a central ducted heat pump gets $1,500, a furnace heat pump add-on gets $1,000, a geothermal heat pump gets $2,000, and mini-split heads get $400 for a single unit or $350 per head for a multizone system. These figures come from an audit-based program, so an energy evaluation comes first.

### What if I already applied for OHPA before it closed?

Follow the dates and instructions in your own approval letter rather than either public program page, since the New Brunswick and federal pages currently list different document and completion deadlines. If your letter does not spell out a deadline, contact NB Power directly to confirm which timeline applies to your file before you miss a submission window.
