# The OHPA Oil to Heat Pump Program Is Closed: What Southeast New Brunswick Homeowners Do Now

The Oil to Heat Pump Affordability Program, known as OHPA, has stopped taking new applications. If you were waiting on that money to help pay for a heat pump and deal with your oil tank at the same time, that window has closed. It does not close off your choices. You can still convert with help from the provincial programs that remain, keep the oil furnace running on an insurable tank, or plan a straight tank replacement on your own schedule.

The dates are where this gets confusing, because the federal OHPA page and New Brunswick's program page list different ones. ## OHPA Is Closed: The New Brunswick Dates

In New Brunswick, OHPA was not run straight from Ottawa. NB Power delivered it on behalf of Natural Resources Canada through its existing SaveEnergyNB programs. The [New Brunswick OHPA page on saveenergynb.ca](https://www.saveenergynb.ca/for-home/oil-to-heat-pump-affordability/) says the federal government closed registration on June 30, 2026.

If you enrolled before July 1, 2026, that page gives you two more dates. All required paperwork had to be submitted by October 1, 2026 to be eligible for an advance payment, and applications received after that are not eligible for advance funding. Then every upgrade and the final energy evaluation must be finished by March 31, 2027. Miss that last one and any advance payment you received has to be paid back to NB Power.

You may have seen other dates online. Natural Resources Canada's [federal OHPA page](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/energy-efficiency/home-energy-efficiency/canada-greener-homes-initiative/oil-heat-pump-affordability-program) says July 31, 2026 was the last day to apply, with January 31, 2027 as the deadline to upload final receipts and the attestation form. Read the top of that page, though. It says its information is for applicants in Alberta, Manitoba, the Northwest Territories, Quebec and Saskatchewan, and it sends New Brunswick residents to their provincial program because the details vary. So for a New Brunswick file, the saveenergynb.ca dates are the ones to plan around.

Even then, if you already applied, the safest move is to open your own approval email or letter and go by the dates on it. Program pages get edited after files are approved. Your approval will not change because a web page did, and if the two ever disagree, call NB Power and ask which one governs your file before you book any work.

## What OHPA Paid For

For anyone who missed the details before registration closed, the idea was simple. OHPA helped lower to median income households that heat with oil switch to an eligible electric heat pump. The federal page describes the income test as a household income at or below the median household after-tax income, and it offers an upfront payment of up to $10,000 in the provinces where the federal government delivers the program alone.

New Brunswick's version was bigger on paper. The saveenergynb.ca page describes an advance payment of up to $15,000, based on approved contractor quotes, paid once your primary residence and household income documents were reviewed and your file was approved. If the project cost more than that, the homeowner paid the difference. Natural Resources Canada's [page for participating provinces](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/energy-efficiency/home-energy-efficiency/canada-greener-homes-initiative/applicants-participating-provinces-territories) explains the higher figure as up to $5,000 more, where a province agreed to match the increased funding.

Both the federal and the New Brunswick pages list the safe removal of your oil tank as an eligible cost. The federal page adds two points worth knowing. The program itself does not require the tank to come out, but your province may require the removal of a tank that is no longer in use, and checking that is on you. That is the one piece of this program that touched our side of the job.

## If You Already Applied, Here Is What Happens Next

If you enrolled before the cutoff and your file was approved, the program is not asking you to do anything differently than it always did. The New Brunswick page lays out the steps: work with a qualified contractor, get your advance payment once your quotes are approved, complete the installation and any other upgrades from your energy evaluation, have your contractor fill in the attestation form, keep every invoice, and finish the final energy evaluation by March 31, 2027. What changes now is that there is no room to register late or add a project that was not already in the system.

If your file included tank removal as an eligible cost, it usually makes sense to schedule it for after the heat pump is running, not before. ## What Is Still on the Table in New Brunswick

OHPA closing does not mean every door in the province just shut. The New Brunswick OHPA page itself points oil-heated households to two programs that are still running.

NB Power's [Enhanced Energy Savings Program](https://www.saveenergynb.ca/en/for-home/enhanced-energy-savings-program/) is still taking registrations. Its page says households with a combined income under $70,000 may qualify for free upgrades, carried out by an NB Power-approved contractor of your choice, and that the program can support a switch from oil heating to a more efficient system. You register with a copy of the most recent Notice of Assessment for each adult living in the home.

For households over $70,000, the [Total Home Energy Savings Program](https://www.saveenergynb.ca/en/for-home/total-home/) is the evaluation-based route. A Certified Energy Advisor evaluates the home first, you have 9 months from that evaluation to complete the recommended upgrades, and rebates follow once the advisor verifies the work. Registrations for the oil to heat pump advance payment through Total Home closed June 30, 2026. The standard program still takes registrations, though, and SaveEnergyNB says Total Home rebates of up to $2,000 are still offered for mini-split, air-source, geothermal, multi-zone and furnace add-on heat pumps.

Neither route puts an advance payment in your account before the work starts the way an approved OHPA file did. They are still worth checking before you assume your conversion has to wait. The federal page does not announce any new intake, and it says the program is moving to Environment and Climate Change Canada.

## No Program Pays You to Swap One Oil Tank for Another

This is worth saying plainly. OHPA, the Enhanced Energy Savings Program and the Total Home Energy Savings Program are all built around the same idea: getting a home off oil heat, or at least making the heating system more efficient. None of the program pages we checked offers money to replace a worn out oil tank with a newer oil tank while you keep burning oil the same way you always have.

If your tank failed an inspection, your insurer sent a letter, or it is simply old, and a heat pump conversion is not something you're doing this year, that is a normal and reasonable call. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is OHPA still accepting new applications?

No. New Brunswick's program page says the federal government closed registration on June 30, 2026. The federal OHPA page, which covers the provinces where Ottawa delivers the program on its own, lists July 31, 2026 as the last day to apply there. Either way, no new applications are being taken, and the federal page does not announce a new intake.

### I already applied and was approved. What deadline do I follow?

Go by the dates on your own approval first. For New Brunswick files, the saveenergynb.ca page set October 1, 2026 for submitting the paperwork needed for an advance payment and March 31, 2027 to finish every upgrade and the final energy evaluation. The January 31, 2027 date on the federal page applies where the federal government delivers the program alone, and that page sends New Brunswick residents to the provincial program.

### Can I still get help paying for a heat pump in New Brunswick without OHPA?

Possibly. NB Power's Enhanced Energy Savings Program offers free upgrades, including support to switch off oil heat, to households with combined income under $70,000. Above that, the Total Home Energy Savings Program starts with a home energy evaluation, and SaveEnergyNB says Total Home rebates of up to $2,000 for eligible heat pumps are still offered. Neither pays in advance the way OHPA did, but both are worth a call.

### Does OHPA closing mean I have to remove my oil tank now?

No. The program closing does not create a removal requirement on its own, and the federal page says OHPA never required removal. If tank removal was one of your eligible costs under an approved file, it stays part of your paperwork and your deadline. If you were never approved, the tank decision is yours and depends on whether you keep oil as backup or go fully electric.

### Is there any program that pays to replace an old oil tank with a new oil tank?

Not that any of the program pages we checked describe. OHPA and New Brunswick's Enhanced and Total Home programs are all structured around switching away from oil heat or improving efficiency, not swapping one oil tank for another.
