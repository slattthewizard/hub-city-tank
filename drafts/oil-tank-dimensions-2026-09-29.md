# Oil Tank Dimensions: Sizes, Capacity, and Will a New One Fit

Before you can answer "will a new oil tank fit," you need to know what you're comparing. Oil tank dimensions change with capacity, with the brand, and with the tank's shape, so two tanks that hold about the same litres can need very different spots in your basement or yard.

The short version, from the manufacturers' own pages: Granby's EcoGard steel tanks are all 47 inches tall and 23 inches wide, and they get longer as they get bigger (45, 60 and 72 inches for the 680, 910 and 1,135 litre models). Roth's double-wall tanks sit on a squarer base about 28 to 30 inches wide and grow taller instead, from 44 inches up to 68 inches. ## Oil Tank Capacity vs Oil Tank Dimensions: Two Different Questions

Capacity tells you how much oil a tank holds. Dimensions tell you how much room it needs. They're related but not the same thing, and mixing them up is how a homeowner ends up with a tank that holds the right amount of oil but won't go down the basement stairs.

A tank's rated capacity also isn't quite what you get to use. Roth's [installation manual](https://www.roth-america.com/app/uploads/2021/02/DWT_Installation_Manual_2023.pdf) explains why: the delivery driver stops filling when the vent alarm goes quiet, which leaves some room at the top, and the manual says code requires an air space of at least six inches or 5% of the tank's volume so the oil has room to expand as it warms. So the oil you can actually store is a bit below the number on the label. That's normal.

Manufacturers also publish capacity in litres, imperial gallons and sometimes US gallons for the same tank, which is where most of the confusion starts.

## Litres, Imperial Gallons, and US Gallons: Why the Numbers Don't Match

Canadian tank makers label residential tanks in litres, with imperial gallons as the traditional second unit. American listings use US gallons, which are smaller: one imperial gallon is about 4.55 litres and one US gallon is about 3.79 litres. That single fact explains most of the mismatched numbers you'll find when you search "275 gallon oil tank."

[Granby Industries](https://www.granbyindustries.com/product-category/residential-tanks/), a tank manufacturer based in Cowansville, Quebec, lists its residential lines in litres and imperial gallons: the EcoGard double-bottom steel tank in 680, 910 and 1,135 litres (150, 200 and 250 imperial gallons), a 2-in-1 double-wall tank in 720 and 1,000 litres (158 and 220 imperial gallons), and fibreglass tanks in roughly the same 680 to 1,137 litre band. Roth lists its double-wall line in US gallons, with the 1,000 litre model shown as 275 gallons.

Here's how the common sizes convert:

| Litres | Imperial gallons | US gallons (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| 680 | 150 | 180 |
| 910 | 200 | 240 |
| 1,000 | 220 | 264 |
| 1,135 | 250 | 300 |

Notice that 1,000 litres works out to about 264 US gallons, not 275. Roth's gallon figures are rounded nominal sizes (a true 275 US gallons would be about 1,040 litres), so treat them as labels rather than exact volumes. The practical takeaway: compare litres first. If a listing only gives gallons, check whether it means imperial or US before you assume a "275 gallon tank" and a 910 litre tank are the same size. They aren't. The 910 litre tank is about 240 US gallons.

## Oil Tank Dimensions by Model: What the Manufacturers Publish

### Granby EcoGard (steel, double bottom)

Granby's [EcoGard product page](https://www.granbyindustries.com/products/ecogard-double-bottom-oil-tank/) lists these dimensions for its oblong steel tank on legs:

| Capacity | Height | Width | Length | Empty weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 680 L (150 imp gal) | 47 in | 23 in | 45 in | 235 lb |
| 910 L (200 imp gal) | 47 in | 23 in | 60 in | 290 lb |
| 1,135 L (250 imp gal) | 47 in | 23 in | 72 in | 335 lb |

The height and width never change. Going up a size adds 12 to 15 inches of length. If your current steel tank is one of these shapes and you're staying in the same capacity, the replacement will usually drop into the same footprint. Going from 910 to 1,135 litres means finding another foot of wall.

Granby's [capacity chart for vertical tanks](https://www.granbyindustries.com/app/uploads/2026/04/GranbyInd_Capacity-Chart_VerticalTanks-1.pdf) is linked from that product page, and it's a handy tool for a different job: converting a dipstick reading into litres. It shows the 200 imperial gallon tank reaching about 921 litres at an oil depth of 46 inches, and the 150 imperial gallon tank topping out at about 689 litres at 42 inches. Granby notes the chart is accurate to about plus or minus 2%.

### Roth DWT (double wall, upright)

[Roth's specification page](https://www.roth-america.com/product/oil-storage-tanks/double-wall-heating-oil-tank/) lists its double-wall line this way. The last column is Roth's minimum ceiling height for installing each model.

| Model | Capacity | Length | Width | Height | Min. height needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DWT 400L | 400 L (110 US gal) | 29 in (74 cm) | 28 in (72 cm) | 44 in (112 cm) | 49 in (125 cm) |
| DWT 620L | 620 L (165 US gal) | 29 in (74 cm) | 28 in (72 cm) | 61 in (155 cm) | 66 in (168 cm) |
| DWT 1000L | 1,000 L (275 US gal) | 43 in (110 cm) | 28 in (72 cm) | 61 in (155 cm) | 66 in (168 cm) |
| DWT 1000LH | 1,000 L (275 US gal) | 51 in (130 cm) | 30 in (76 cm) | 54 in (137 cm) | 60 in (152 cm) |
| DWT 1500L | 1,500 L (400 US gal) | 64 in (163 cm) | 30 in (77 cm) | 68 in (173 cm) | 76 in (193 cm) |

The 400 and 620 litre models share a footprint and only gain height. The 1000LH holds the same as the 1000L but is built lower and longer, which is the variant to ask about when ceiling height is your limit rather than floor space.

## Oblong vs Upright: Same Litres, Different Footprint

Put the two brands side by side at about the same capacity and the trade-off is plain. A 910 litre EcoGard needs a spot 60 inches long and 23 inches deep, and it's 47 inches tall. A 1,000 litre Roth needs only 43 by 28 inches of floor, but it stands 61 inches tall and Roth asks for 66 inches of clear height to install it.

So an upright double-wall tank can fit a tight furnace room that has no five-foot run of wall, while an oblong steel tank can be the only thing that clears a low basement with ductwork close to the joists. Granby also lists horizontal fibreglass models (682 and 909 litres), built to lie on their side. For those, get the spec sheet for the exact model before you plan around it.

Weight matters too, mostly for the base. Roth's installation manual works out that a full 1,000 litre tank weighs close to a ton, which is why it wants full support under each leg and says masonry blocks and pavers won't do. It also says a new level pad poured on an existing floor should extend six inches past the tank on every exposed side. ## Will a New Oil Tank Fit? The Access Questions That Matter

Roth's installation manual puts the first rule bluntly: make sure the tank will fit through every door, stair, hall and turn on the way to its spot, because it can't be taken apart to squeeze through. Before you settle on a model, walk the path and measure four things.

**The narrowest opening on the route.** Compare it to the tank's published width and height, not its capacity, and ask the installer which way the tank will be carried through. Then check every turn at a landing, where the tank's length has to swing around.

**The clear height along the way and at the spot.** Low bulkheads, ductwork and stair headroom are where an upright tank's extra height bites, even when its footprint is small. For a Roth, use the "min. height needed" figure, not the tank height.

**The floor space at the final spot, plus working room.** Roth's manual asks for at least 2 inches between the tank and any wall, because its sides can bulge outward up to 2 inches when full. It also cites CSA B139 as requiring 460 mm (18 inches) of service room along one side and one end, recommends 24 inches clear above the tank, and lists a 2 foot separation from the heating appliance for Canadian installs. Those are the figures Roth's manual gives for its tanks; your installer works to the code edition in force and the tank maker's instructions for the model you choose.

**For an outdoor tank, the pad and the distances to walls, windows, doors and vents.** Our [CSA B139 explainer](/blog/csa-b139-oil-tank-code/) covers where those rules come from.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the dimensions of a standard 900 litre oil tank?

It depends on the model. Granby's 910 litre (200 imperial gallon) EcoGard steel tank is 47 inches tall, 23 inches wide and 60 inches long. Roth's closest size is a 1,000 litre double-wall tank at 61 inches tall on a 43 by 28 inch base. There's no single footprint for "a 900 litre tank," so confirm the model before assuming it fits your spot.

### Is a 275 gallon oil tank the same size as a 1,000 litre tank?

Close, but check which gallon is meant. Roth labels its 1,000 litre tank as 275 US gallons, a rounded figure: 1,000 litres is about 264 US gallons or 220 imperial gallons. A Canadian 910 litre tank is 200 imperial gallons, or about 240 US gallons, so it's smaller than a true 275 US gallon tank. Compare litres first.

### How do I know if a new oil tank will fit through my basement door?

Measure the narrowest point on the path from outside to the tank's final spot, including the turns at landings, and compare it to the tank's published dimensions, not its capacity. Roth's manual notes its tanks can't be taken apart to fit. An upright tank has a smaller footprint but more height, so check headroom under stairs, ducts and bulkheads too.

### Do bigger oil tanks need more clearance around them?

Capacity isn't the main driver. The service room and separation distances come from CSA B139 and the maker's instructions, and they apply whatever the tank size. Roth's manual, for example, calls for 2 inches from walls, cites 18 inches of service room along one side and one end, and recommends 24 inches above the tank. A bigger tank simply needs a bigger spot to hold all of that.

### What is the difference between a tank's rated capacity and how much oil it holds?

The rated capacity is the label figure. In use, the driver stops when the vent alarm goes quiet, and Roth's manual says code requires an air space of at least six inches or 5% of the tank's volume for the oil to expand. So the most you can store is a little less than the label says, which is normal and not a sign of a problem.
